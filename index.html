Twelve words on paper

The phrase opens your vault on any device. You prove it works before your first save, and you can rehearse it again from Settings whenever you like.

Three sheets, any two

Print the family kit and keep the sheets in different places: one at home, one with a sibling, one in a drawer at work. Any two open the vault. One alone reveals nothing.

Someone you choose

A trusted person can ask for access in an emergency. Nothing opens until a waiting period you set has passed and you approve it. You can refuse at any time before then.

The hard questions

Locked out

No. We never hold your keys, so there is nothing for us to reset. That is the point, and it is also the risk. Lockhearth asks you to prove your recovery works before it saves anything, checks your iCloud copy every month, and offers the printed kit and a trusted person as extra ways back.

New phone

Sign in with the same Apple Account and turn on iCloud Keychain. Your vault key arrives through iCloud Keychain and your encrypted files through iCloud. If the key has not arrived, the app waits and shows that it is waiting. It never shows an empty vault as if your files were gone. Your 12 words open it either way.

Storage

On your devices and in your own iCloud, encrypted before they leave the device. Apple stores the encrypted data; neither Apple nor we can read it. Lockhearth has no server that receives your documents.

Paying

You keep everything. You can read, open and export every file. You just cannot add beyond the free limits or use family features until you renew. A lifetime purchase stays lifetime.

Lost words

While you can still open the app, make a new phrase from Settings. The old one stops working for new data. If the paper may have been stolen, Lockhearth can also re-encrypt your vault so the old words open nothing.
